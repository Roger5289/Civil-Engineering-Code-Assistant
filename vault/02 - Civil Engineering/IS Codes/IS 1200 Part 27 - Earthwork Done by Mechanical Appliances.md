# IS 1200 (Part 27):1992 — Method of Measurement: Earthwork Done by Mechanical Appliances

Part of the [[IS 1200 - Method of Measurement Index|IS 1200]] series. Companion/alternative to [[IS 1200 Part 1 - Earthwork]] — Part 1 covers manual excavation/carriage/filling; this Part covers the **same work done by machine** (tractors, dozers, scrapers, graders, shovel loaders, drag lines, dumpers, etc.), written because the manual-method rules left real gaps once mechanization became common. Source: `...\1200_27.pdf` (5 pages). Extracted text: `<local extracted text>\IS 1200\IS-1200-Part27-fulltext.txt`.

## General rules for this Part (Cl 2)
- Dimensions to nearest 0.01 m — **but any dimension >25 m is measured to the nearest 0.1 m** (a precision relaxation unique to this Part, reflecting the scale of machine-worked earthwork). Areas/volumes to nearest 0.01 m²/m³.
- Work-measured-separately: water/foul/tides/**snow** (matches Part 1's list, i.e. this Part restores the full list rather than Part 16's trimmed version).
- **Not measured separately** (deemed included): setting out; site clearance; unauthorized battering/benching; excavation for planking/strutting insertion; removing slips/falls (unless otherwise specified); rainwater bailing/pumping; **bailing/pumping when measured underground**; **slinging pipes/electric cables etc. met during excavation** (this last item is Part-27-specific — not in Part 1's list, since mechanical excavation is more likely to encounter buried services needing this handling).

## Classification of soils (Cl 3) — narrower/different bands than Part 1
- **Soil** — a broad category here (unlike Part 1's finer soft/hard split): various soil types, mud concrete below ground, shingle, river/nallah-bed boulders, road/path soling+hard core, macadam surface (any description), lime concrete, stone masonry below ground level, soft conglomerate and laterite **when the stone can be detached with picks and shovel.**
- **Mud** — soil/water mixture, fluid or weak-solid state.
- **Foul condition** — soil mixed with sewerage or night soil.
- **Rock** — quarried/split with mechanical implements; includes limestone, plain/reinforced/prestressed cement concrete below ground level. If blasting is needed to excavate it, blasting may be used for measurement purposes.
- **Hard rock** — excavatable by machine but needs blasting/chiselling/edging or another specified method; **if no such method is specified, it's treated as ordinary rock** for measurement purposes.

## Method of measurement (Cl 4)
- **Base unit: m³** unless stated otherwise.
- **Levels taken with staff and level, recorded correct to 5 mm**; cutting depths/level heights calculated correct to 5 mm; cubical contents to the nearest decimal place in m³.
- **Mixed ordinary rock + soil:** the entire excavation is recorded as one quantity. **Excavated ordinary rock is stacked, separately measured, and reduced by 50% for voids** to arrive at the payable ordinary-rock quantity. **The difference** between total excavation and that rock quantity **is payable as ordinary-soil excavation.** *(This is genuinely different from Part 1's mixed-strata method, which uses tell-tale cross-sections rather than a flat 50% stack-void reduction — a real methodological divergence between the manual and mechanical Parts, not an inconsistency to "fix.")*
- **Where measuring from borrow pits/cuttings isn't practical:** work volume is derived from the **fill** instead — actual fill measured from original-ground levels (taken before work start, after site clearance) versus levels after compaction to the specified density (density must be pre-specified).
- **Embankments:** m³, incl. slope formation. Layer thickness stated if deposited in layers; consolidation method described. Measured in **1.5 m successive stages**, stating commencing level (same convention as Part 1).
  - **Mechanical backfilling** — calculated by **deducting the below-ground structure's volume from the originally-measured excavation volume** (a formula specific to this Part; Part 1 has no equivalent backfill-by-subtraction rule).
- **Lead:** measured over the specified or shortest-practicable machinery route. **>500 m: measured in 500 m units.** **>5 km: measured in 1 km units** (half-km-and-above rounds up, less-than-half-km is ignored) — note this differs from Part 1's finer 50 m/500 m banding, reflecting machinery's coarser practical routing. Travel distance = centre-of-gravity of excavated ground to centre-of-gravity of the dumping ground, by a reasonable path.
- Consolidated spoil heaps (settled over time) — stated and measured separately.
- **Lift: NOT measured separately at all in mechanical earthwork** — a direct, explicit divergence from Part 1, where lift is a real separately-measured item (1.5 m bands). Mechanical excavation's lift cost is treated as absorbed into the base rate.
- **Planking and strutting** (to uphold excavated-face sides) — m² of face supported; use+waste of all timber (incl. struts/boards), fixing+removal included. Permanently-left-in-position planking/strutting — measured separately.
- **Removing trees:** trees **exceeding 30 cm girth** (measured 1 m above ground) — by number, deemed to include removal + **depositing within 50 m** (Part 1's equivalent clause has different girth bands — 300mm–1000mm enumerated as one item, >1000mm stating girth — so this Part's simpler "just >30cm girth, all one class" rule is a genuine simplification worth noting if cross-referencing the two Parts on the same job).
