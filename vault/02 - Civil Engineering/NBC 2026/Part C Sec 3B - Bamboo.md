# Part C, Section 3B — Bamboo

Structural design and construction with bamboo: species selection, grading, permissible stresses, joints, bamboo-as-reinforcement in concrete, panel products, and traditional construction details. PDF pages 609–658 (Annex + List of Standards ends p.657; p.658 blank; Part C Sec 4 Masonry begins p.659). Fourth revision (2026) of NBC; supersedes the 2016 edition of this subsection.

## Scope (Cl 1, p.613)
Covers general principles for structural design of bamboo (round, split, glued-laminated) and bamboo-based panels (adhesive- or mechanically-fastened): minimum strength data, dimensional stability, grading, traditional joints, and site/off-site construction practices. **Not covered:** limit-state design, and scientific/analytical design of joint fastenings (p.613).

Reference to Part F 'Fire and Life Safety' for fire protection of bamboo buildings, and to Part B 'Building Materials' for material quality (p.610, p.911).

## Key definitions (Cl 2, pp.613–616)
Selected terms with engineering relevance (full glossary runs 2.1–2.4 at pp.613–616):
- **Characteristic load** — value with 95% probability of not being exceeded during the structure's life (p.614).
- **Characteristic strength** — strength below which ≤5% of test results fall (p.614).
- **Permissible stress** — ultimate stress ÷ factor of safety = working stress (p.615).
- **Bundle-column** — 3+ culms bound together as one unit with wire/strap fastenings (p.614).
- **Mean outer diameter** — mean of 4 readings (2 perpendicular diameters at each end) (p.615).
- **Slenderness ratio** — length of member ÷ least radius of gyration (p.615).
- Wet / Inside / Outside location definitions govern which permissible-stress modification applies (p.615–616).

## Symbols (Cl 3, pp.616–617)
A = cross-section area; D = mean outer diameter; d = inner diameter = D−2w; E = MOE in bending; fc = calc. axial compressive stress; fcp = permissible compressive stress along fibres; I = moment of inertia = (π/64)[D⁴−(D−2w)⁴]; ℓ = unsupported column length; m = moisture %; r = radius of gyration = √(I/A); R = MOR; w = mean wall thickness; Z = section modulus = (π/32)[(D⁴−(D−2w)⁴)/D]; δ = deflection.

## 4. Materials (pp.617–620)
- **Species (4.1, p.617):** >100 species native to India, 20 systematically tested, 16 recommended for structural round-form use. Matured bamboo ≥4 years age, used only after ≥6 weeks post-felling (4.1.1). Must be treated per good practice [C-3B(1)] (4.1.2). Broken/damaged/collapsed/bore-holed/checks >3mm deep bamboo rejected (4.1.4).
- **Grouping (4.2, p.617):** 16 species classified into Groups A/B/C by MOE (bending, green) and MOR — see **Table 2** below.
- **Moisture (4.3, p.617–618):** Strength rises exponentially as moisture drops; fibre saturation point ≈25% moisture. Seasoning target: 15% moisture before use. Curve at **Fig. 1 — Moisture Strength Relationship of Bambusa Nutans (p.618)**.
- **Grading (4.4, pp.618–619):**
  - By mean outer diameter, Groups A/B (10mm steps): Special Grade A 100–130mm · Special Grade B 70–100mm · Grade I 50–70mm · Grade II 30–50mm · Grade III <30mm.
  - Group C (20mm steps): Grade I 80–100mm · Grade II 60–80mm · Grade III <60mm.
  - Minimum culm length: 4.5m preferred (4.4.2.2).
  - **Taper:** ≤5.8mm/m (0.58%, i.e. 1:170) (4.4.3).
  - **Curvature:** ≤75mm over 6m length (4.4.4).
  - **Wall thickness:** ≥8mm preferred for load-bearing members (4.4.5).
- **Durability (4.5, pp.619–620):** Natural durability 12–36 months depending on species/climate; 1–2 yrs if exposed+ground contact, 2–5 yrs if covered and out of ground contact. Split bamboo deteriorates faster than round. Fire safety per 7.3.4 (4.5.2).

## 5. Permissible Stresses (pp.620–624)
- Ultimate stress values determined per accepted standard [C-3B(2)]; divided by safety factors for variability, load duration, grade, location, dynamic loading (5.1).
- **Strength factors (5.1.1):** extreme fibre stress in beams ÷4; MOE ÷4.5; max compressive stress parallel to grain ÷3.5.
- **Table 3 — Safe permissible stresses for 16 species** (extreme fibre bending stress, MOE, allowable compressive stress), Groups A/B/C — pp.623–624.
- **Load-duration modification factors (5.3):** permanent loads ×1.0; imposed/medium-term (permanent+temporary) ×1.25; short-term (permanent+temporary+wind) ×1.50.

## 6. Design Considerations (pp.624–628)
- **6.1 Basic requirements:** design/construction by appropriately skilled personnel; adequate reliability, durability, maintainability; worst load combination; load path traced roof→foundation→soil; wind and seismic forces **not** considered simultaneously (6.1(j)). Loads per Part C Sec 1 'Loads and Load Effects' — note permeable bamboo walls/floors/roofs affect internal pressure and hence wind load factor (6.1(h)).
- **6.4 Net section:** least net sectional area used for load capacity; gross section for intermediate/long columns.
- **6.5 Structural components:** roofing, flooring, shear walls, wall panelling, beams, piles, columns, arches; trusses/framed skeletons preferred.
- **6.6 Flexural members:** designed by beam theory; account for creep (double dead-load deflection or MOE safety factor); shear check required if beam length <25× diameter at small end (6.6.5).
- **6.7 Columns:** designed via full-scale buckling tests OR calculation. **6.7.3:** Euler buckling with 90% moment-of-inertia reduction to account for taper (<1:170).
- **6.8 Bunching:** single culm not optimal — bunching used for beams/columns (see Fig. 9D/9E). Precautions (6.8.1): min. 100mm drill-to-end distance; drill points within 30mm of node; interference fit 0.25–0.30mm (wall ≥8mm) or 0.20–0.25mm (wall <8mm).
- **6.9 Trusses:** truss height ≥0.15×span (triangular/pitched) or ≥0.10×span (rectangular/parallel) (6.9.4). Pin-ended joint trusses **Fig. 2A**; gusset joint trusses **Fig. 2B** (p.628). Full-scale tested trusses (4m span, wire-bound + bamboo pins) achieved FoS ≥5 with acceptable deflection (6.9.8, p.627).
  - **6.9.9 Bamboo Mat Corrugated Sheets (BMCS)** — sustainable roofing, must comply with [C-3B(5)].
  - **6.9.10 Bamboo Mat Ridge Cap (BMRC)** — companion ridge covering for BMCS roofs.

## 7. Joints and Construction Practices (pp.628–636)
- Joint diagrams: **Fig. 3** spliced joints — full/half-lapped (p.629); **Fig. 4** bearing joints — saddle, pegged/tied, dowelled/tied, purlin-to-rafter (p.630); **Fig. 5** nut-bolt joinery (p.630); **Fig. 6** gusset-plated joints — lashed, truss chord/web, plywood/timber plates (p.631); **Fig. 7** other joints — bolted with MS arc washers, horned+rope, steel band, hardwood dowel, solid/split bamboo pins (p.632).
- **7.2.1 Traditional practices:** lap joints (full/half, ≥1 internode overlap), butt joints (side plates), sleeves/inserts, scarf joints (1:4–1:6 slope) — lengthening joints, p.633. Bearing joints: saddle joints (seat near node to reduce splitting), angled joints, p.633.
- **7.2.2 Improved practices:** plywood/timber gusset plates with bamboo pins or MS bolts; wooden inserts or steel band-clamps to reinforce ends before jointing.
- **7.2.3 Fastening:** wire-bound joints — 1.60mm (16 SWG) GI wire typical for tight lashing (general), 2.00mm (14 SWG) for wire-bound joints with pins, ≥2 drilled holes/piece (p.633–634); fish plates ≥25mm hardwood or ≥12mm structural plywood, fixed with solid bamboo pins (7.2.3.2).
- **7.2.7** Metal fasteners must be corrosion-resistant or protected.
- **7.3.4 Fire retardant treatment (formula):** ammonium phosphate 3 parts : boric acid 3 parts : copper sulphate 1 part : zinc chloride 5 parts : sodium dichromate 3 parts : water 100 parts, + drops of conc. HCl to dissolve precipitated salts (p.635).
- **7.3.5 Foundations:** bamboo on preformed concrete footings, incorporated into concrete footings, or out-of-ground-contact strip footings — **Fig. 8A/8B/8C** (p.635); metallic strap/C-clamp/metal tube bundle-column foundations — **Fig. 9A–9E** (p.636).

## 8. Bamboo as Reinforcement in Concrete (pp.637–639)
- Tensile strength of some species in direct tension ≈ steel at yield; averages 1,400–2,000 kg/cm².
- **Table 4 — Strength characteristics, MS steel vs bamboo (green) vs mass concrete** (p.637): MOE — steel 2.1×10⁶, bamboo 175,750–196,640, kgf/cm²; tensile stress — steel 1,265.5–1,400, bamboo 158 (bending-tension); compressive stress — bamboo (D. strictus) 105, concrete 500; bond stress — bamboo 3.5(green)–5.6(dry) kgf/cm² (reported, possibly up to 10), concrete 40–100; shear stress — bamboo 115–180, concrete 50.
- **Table 5 — Balanced-section design data, M15 (1:2:4) concrete** (p.637): modular ratio — steel 18, bamboo 1–1.4; neutral axis depth from top — steel 0.4d, bamboo 0.25d–0.254d; lever arm — steel 0.87d, bamboo 0.915d–0.917d; MOR constant Q (=BM/bd²) — steel 137, bamboo 86.
- **8.2–8.3:** Reinforcement strips 20–25mm wide × 9mm thick, split from halved/quartered culms; triangular lugs at nodes improve bond. Mandatory treatment: pressure-impregnation (warm coal-tar creosote) + hot bitumen (80/100 grade) water-repellent coat + coarse sand.
- **Table 6 — Strength properties of split bamboo (green), 5 species** (bending MOR, MOE, compression parallel, shear) — p.638.
- **8.4 Placement (see Fig. 10, p.642):** main reinforcement laid edgewise in twin strips; subsidiary laid flatwise alternating smooth/rough face; negative-moment strips = span/6 length; split-bamboo or steel rod for diagonal shear reinforcement; bound with GI wire/nylon/bamboo-cane string.

## 9. Panel Products, Walling, Flooring, Roofing (pp.639–646)
- **9.1.1** Panel materials: bamboo mat board [C-3B(8)], bamboo mat veneer composite [C-3B(9)], BMCS/BMRC [C-3B(5)], bamboo-jute composite sheets [C-3B(10)].
- **9.1.2 Reconstituted wood from destructured bamboo** — **Table 7** (p.640) compares mechanical properties (density, MOR, tensile/compressive strength, Izod impact, shear, MOE, screw-holding) of two board grades vs Shorea robusta (sal), Tectona grandis (teak), and raw D. strictus.
- **9.2 Structural usage:** BMB/BMVC high in-plane rigidity → wall panelling, composite beams/floor decks, stress-skin panels; BMCS waterproof, decay/termite/fire resistant.
- **Table 8 — Permissible safe working stresses, BMB & BMVC** (p.643): tensile 8.5 (BMB) / 10 (BMVC along & across) N/mm²; compressive 10.80 / 13 / 6.5; MOR 19.00 / 19 / 10.5; MOE 3,200 / 4,600 (BMVC along only); modulus of rigidity 4,970 / 1,930.
