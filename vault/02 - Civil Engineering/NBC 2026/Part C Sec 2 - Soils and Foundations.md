# Part C, Section 2 — Soils and Foundations

Geotechnical design principles for building foundations: site investigation, soil classification, shallow foundations (spread/strip, raft, ring, shell), deep foundations (driven/bored piles, precast piles, under-reamed piles, timber piles), combined piled-raft foundations (CPRF — new in this edition), other/special foundations (piers, machine foundations, diaphragm walls), ground improvement, and geotechnical instrumentation. PDF pages 383–492. This edition renames the publication from "NBC" to "National Building Construction Standards (NBCS)" — otherwise this is the fourth revision of the old NBC Part C/Sec 2.

New in this 2026 revision vs 2016: expanded site investigation methods (CPTu, field CBR, cross-hole seismic, GPR, MASW/SASW, DMT, optical/acoustic tele-viewer, in-situ permeability), detailed geotechnical engineering services roles (per IS 19235:2025), and an entirely new clause 9 on **Combined Piled Raft Foundations (CPRF)** — a composite system using both raft and piles together, not previously covered (p. 385).

## 1 Scope (p. 389)
Covers geotechnical design of shallow foundations (strip, combined, raft) and deep foundations (piles) and other systems, ensuring safety/serviceability without exceeding permissible material stresses or soil/rock bearing capacity. Also covers preliminary work and excavation protection.

## 2 Terminology (p. 389–393)
Key definitions: safe bearing capacity, ultimate bearing capacity, allowable bearing pressure (gross/net), factor of safety, shallow/pile/raft foundation types, negative skin friction, under-reamed pile, and (new) full CPRF terminology — angular distortion θ, pile-raft coefficient/load-sharing ratio αCPRF (not valid for αCPRF ≥ 0.9), settlement reduction ratio (SRR). See Fig. 1 (CPRF parameter schematic, p. 394) and Fig. 2 (pile-raft interaction types, p. 395–396).

## 3 Site Investigation (p. 397–411)
**3.2 Stages:** reconnaissance → preliminary → detailed → construction-stage → post-construction-stage investigation (p. 397).

**3.3 Subsurface exploration methods** (p. 399–401): geophysical survey (Table 1, p. 399 — ERT, SRT, CHST/DHST, SASW/MASW, GPR, thermal resistivity), trial pits (up to ~4 m depth, planned 1×1 m to 3×3 m), subsurface soundings (DCPT, SCPT, CPTu), boring (auger up to 6 m by hand; shell-and-auger for deep holes; percussion for bouldery/gravelly strata; rotary for all strata, borehole dia ~100–150 mm), and rock core drilling (NX/HX diamond bit, double-tube core barrel, drilling run 0.75 m, extendable to 1.5 m if recovery >50%; SPT required if recovery is nil).

**3.3.6 Location/depth of investigation:**
- Disposition of boreholes (Table 2, p. 402): 1 per lightly-loaded residential building; grid ≤50 m spacing for buildings ≤50 m height or plan >50 m; minimum 3 boreholes for buildings >50 m height; linear structures 50–500 m spacing; solar plants 1 borehole per 2–5 ha (min. 5/site).
- Depth of investigation (p. 402–403): shallow foundations — 2 to 3× largest footing width below founding level; raft — 1.0–2.0× raft width; piles — ≥5 m or 5× pile diameter beyond estimated termination level (whichever more); embankments — 1.0–2.0× embankment height; wells — 1.5–2.0× well width/diameter.

**3.4 In-situ tests** (p. 404–408): SPT (63.5±0.5 kg hammer, 750±10 mm drop, N = blows for 300 mm penetration after 150 mm seating), vane shear, pressuremeter (PMT), plate load test (PLT), cyclic PLT, block vibration test, DCPT (stop at 35 blows/100mm dry or 20 blows/100mm with slurry), SCPT, CPTu, DMT, electrical resistivity, seismic refraction, downhole/uphole/crosshole seismic, SASW/MASW, GPR, thermal resistivity, in-situ permeability (pump-in: constant head for high-permeability, falling head for low-permeability, percolation for trial pits; pump-out for water-bearing strata), optical/acoustic tele-viewer.

**3.5–3.6 Sampling & reporting** (p. 408–411): UDS collected at 3.0 m intervals or change of strata (whichever lower), staggered 1.5 m with SPT. Lab tests on disturbed/undisturbed/rock samples and chemical analysis (pH, sulphates, chlorides, organic matter) listed in full (p. 409–410). Investigation report must include borelogs, N-value plots, strata summary, liquefaction susceptibility, and — for shallow foundations — net allowable bearing pressure by both shear and settlement criteria.

## 4–5 Classification & Materials (p. 412)
Soil classification per accepted standard [C-2(27)]. Foundation materials (cement, aggregate, steel, timber) must conform to Part B. Concrete per Part C/Sec 5; timber in contact with soil must be treated per good practice [C-2(28)].

## 6 General Requirements for Foundations (p. 412–417)
**6.1 Types covered:** shallow (pad/spread/strip, raft, ring, shell) and deep (driven/bored cast-in-situ, driven precast, precast-in-prebored-holes, under-reamed, timber piles, spun piles for deep marshy soils); other (piers, CPRF, diaphragm walls, machine foundations).

**6.2 Depth of foundations:** minimum 500 mm below natural ground level (p. 413). Must clear zones of seasonal shrink/swell in clays, frost penetration in fine sands/silts, and scour depth.

**6.3 Foundations at different levels** (sloping ground/adjacent footings, p. 413–414): sloping ground surface must not intersect a 30° frustum under the footing; min. horizontal clearance 600 mm (rock) / 900 mm (soil) — see Fig. 4, 5, 6 (p. 414). Adjacent footings at different levels: line between lower edges must not exceed 1V:2H slope.

**6.4–6.9** Seasonal weather effects on clays (maintain ≥6 m from fast-growing trees, p. 415), mining subsidence, landslide-prone area precautions (spread foundations stepped and lapped ≥ foundation thickness or 2× step height, p. 416), inclined strata drainage, varying-thickness strata, spacing between existing/new foundations (min. horizontal spacing = width of wider footing, p. 417).

**6.10–6.12** Preliminary site work: clearance, drainage, setting out (permanent pillars ≥250 mm wide, p. 417), excavation protection (bottom compacted, wet excavations protected with 80 mm C1:5:10 concrete layer, backfill compacted in ≤150 mm layers, p. 418), shoring for deep excavations, and design correction procedures if actual soil conditions differ from assumed during construction.

## 7 Shallow Foundations (p. 418–424)
**7.2.2 Allowable bearing pressure** = lesser of (a) safe bearing capacity from shear strength (FoS = 2.5) or (b) pressure within permissible settlement. Wind/seismic increase allowances (p. 419): if wind-induced pressure <25% of DL+LL pressure, ignore; else limit combined pressure to +25% over allowable. Seismic increase per Part C/Sec 1.

**7.2.3 Settlement — Table 3 (p. 422, verified against pdfplumber table extraction, values match):** permissible total/differential settlement & angular distortion by structure type:

**7.3 Pad/spread/strip foundations** (p. 423–424): min. width B = W + 300 mm (wall foundations); unreinforced spread limited to 1V:½H (masonry), 1V:1H (concrete), 1V:⅔H (lime concrete); min. edge thickness 150 mm.

**7.3.12 Footing thickness — Table 4 (p. 423, cross-checked):** Masonry — greater of 250 mm or 2× max projection. Plain concrete (normal) — greatest of 200 mm / 2× stepped offset / 300 mm on soil; on pile cap different. Plain concrete (lightly loaded) — 150 mm (soil) / 200 mm (pile). RCC — 150 mm (soil) / 300 mm (pile).

**7.4 Raft foundations** (p. 423–427): min. depth ≥1 m. Choice of raft type (flat slab / thickened-under-column / slab-and-beam / cellular) — Fig. 7 (p. 426). Analysis methods: rigid/conventional method (K>0.5, Annex C) vs flexible foundation methods (simplified spring-bed or general plate-theory method for K<0.5).

**7.5–7.6** Ring and shell foundations — refer to good practice [C-2(37)]/[C-2(38)] (not detailed in this Section itself).

## 8 Deep Foundations — Piles (p. 427–442)
**8.2 Driven/bored cast-in-situ concrete piles:** min. grade M25, slump 150–180 mm, min. cement 400 kg/m³ (min. 350 kg/m³ with admixture). Working stress ≤25% of 28-day cube strength.

**8.2.3.3 Pile capacity** via static formula (Annex G), dynamic formula (driven piles), or load test (not earlier than 4 weeks after casting). **Uplift capacity FoS:** 3.0 (no pullout test) / 2.0 (with pullout test).

**8.2.3.6 Spacing:** end-bearing on hard stratum/rock — 2.5× (or 2×) circumscribing-circle diameter; friction piles — ≥3× diameter.

**8.2.3.8–8.2.3.10 Factor of safety, transient loading, overloading:** min. FoS = 2.5 (static formula). Wind-load transient increase: +25% of safe load. Overloading: up to 10% per pile / 10% per group, capped at 25% increase on any single pile (p. 431).

**8.2.3.11 Reinforcement:** min. longitudinal steel 0.4% of shaft cross-section, full length. Min. links/spirals: 8 mm dia, spacing ≤150 mm; stiffener rings 16 mm @ 1.5 m c/c; min. 6 vertical bars, min. 12 mm dia; clear cover ≥50 mm.

**8.2.3.12–13 Pile caps & grade beams:** 45° load dispersion assumption; min. cap overhang beyond outermost pile 150 mm; min. clear cover in cap 60 mm; pile embedment into cap 75 mm. Grade beam min. depth 200 mm; max BM = wl²/30. In expansive soils: 75 mm levelling concrete over 300 mm compacted MSM/gravel layer, width ≥5× beam width (Fig. 8, p. 433).

**8.3 Driven precast piles** (p. 434–436): max length 50× least width (else use segmental piling). Bending moment for handling — **Table 5 (p. 434):** 1-point pickup at 0.293L → 4.3WL; 2-point at 0.207L → 2.2WL; 3-point at 0.145L → 1.05WL. Driving stress formula with efficiency-of-blow factor n (values by P/W ratio and hammer type e, p. 435 — verify exact table against PDF, OCR of the n-value grid is visually ambiguous in extraction).

**8.4 Precast piles in prebored holes** (p. 436–437): as 8.3 except reinforcement design; central 40 mm duct for grouting.

**8.5 Under-reamed piles** (p. 437–441): suited to expansive/black-cotton soils. Bulb dia 2–2.5× shaft dia; bulb spacing ≥1.5× bulb dia; first bulb at ≥2× bulb dia depth; max 4 bulbs above water table, max 2 in high-water-table clay/silt strata (drilling-mud-stabilized strata: max 1 bulb); min. shaft dia 250 mm (300 mm in aggressive/sulphate ground or tremie concreting); max practical depth 10 m without load-test-verified feasibility; min. pile length in expansive soil 3.5 m.

**8.6 Timber piles** (p. 441–442): generally single/two-storey structures in backwater areas, bearing strata <12 m; must be fully submerged to avoid deterioration; length tolerance ±300 mm (≤12 m piles) / ±600 mm (>12 m); heartwood:butt diameter ratio ≥0.8.

## 9 Combined Piled Raft Foundations — CPRF (p. 442–448) — NEW in 2026
Composite system combining pile + raft load transfer; **not applicable** where soft/organic/collapsible/liquefiable soil lies directly beneath the raft, or where top/bottom layer stiffness contrast is very high.

Three design philosophies (p. 445): conventional (piles as group, raft contribution allowance), settlement-control (piles placed strategically to reduce differential settlement — the governing approach for CPRF per 9.3.3.3.1), creep piling (piles operate near 70–80% ultimate capacity).

**Dimensionless design factors — Table 6 (p. 445):** pile slenderness Lp/Dp = 10–100; pile spacing ratio Sp/Dp = 2.5–8; pile-soil stiffness ratio Kps = Ep/Es = 100–10,000; raft aspect ratio Lr/Br = 1–10; raft-soil stiffness ratio Krs = 0.001–10.

**9.3.5 Allowable settlement:** total settlement under gravity load ≤125 mm; max. angular distortion 1/500 (governing value of four measured distortions θIL, θIIL, θIB, θIIB per Fig. 1).

**9.4–9.5** Requires oversight by a qualified geotechnical expert at both design proof-check and construction monitoring stages (inclinometers/piezometers/load cells etc. per Table 7, shared with clause 12).

## 10 Other Foundations / Special Structures (p. 448–449)
**10.1 Pier foundations:** bell (if provided) ≥300 mm thick at edge, sides sloped ≥60° from horizontal, min. dimension 600 mm. Plain concrete piers: height ≤6× least dimension (up to 12× with reduced allowable stress, formula p. 448 — fc' = fc(1.3 − H/20D)). RC piers >18× least dimension: reduced max load P' = P(1.5 − H/36D).

**10.2** Machine foundations & diaphragm walls — refer to good practice [C-2(55)] (not detailed here).

## 11 Ground Improvement (p. 449–450)
General principles only in-section; **Annex L (p. 476–483)** is a large comparative table of methods — see below. Specific cross-references: stone columns [C-2(57)], preloading with vertical drains [C-2(58)], geosynthetics [C-2(59)].

## 12 Geotechnical Instrumentation (p. 450–451)
**Table 7 (p. 450–451, cross-checked):** inclinometers (lateral deflection — excavations, slopes, lateral pile tests), piezometers (pore pressure), vibration monitors/seismographs (adjacent structure monitoring during construction/blasting/pile driving), strain gauges (piles, struts), load cells (rock bolts, anchors, struts, pile load tests), pressure pads/cells (embankments, rafts, retaining walls), extensometers (deformation), permanent settlement markers, crackmeters (bi-directional static load tests).
