# Part E, Section 3 — Solid Waste Management

Covers solid waste management systems for buildings and their built environments: sorting/segregation, refuse chute and dumb-waiter systems, per-capita waste quantity assessment, and treatment methods (biological, thermal, landfill). Part of "Plumbing Services (Including Solid Waste Management)."

## 1 Scope (p. 2689)
Covers solid waste management systems, assessment of per capita solid waste quantities, and treatment of solid waste within buildings, building complexes, and their built environments.

## 2 Terminology (p. 2689–2690)
Key terms: Anaerobic Digestion, Authorization, Biodegradable Substance, Biomethanation, Collection, Composting, Construction and Demolition Waste, Disposal, Generator of Wastes, Landfilling, Leachate, Municipal Authority, Municipal Solid Waste, Operator of a Facility, Pelletization, Processing, Recycling, Segregation, Storage, Transportation, Vermi-composting.

## 3 General (p. 2690–2692)
- **3.2** Waste categorized by source: domestic, industrial, institutional/commercial, horticulture.
- **3.3** Waste categorized by handling/disposal suitability: garbage, municipal waste, hazardous waste, bio-medical waste (est. **1.5 kg/bed/day**, ~25% of hospital waste), plastics (avoid down-cycling), e-waste.
- **3.4** Must comply with: Environment (Protection) Act 1986; Solid Waste Management Rules 2016; Bio-Medical Waste Management Rules 2016; E-Waste (Management) Rules 2022; Battery Waste Management Rules 2022; Hazardous and Other Wastes (Management and Transboundary Movement) Rules 2016; Plastic Waste Management (Amendment) Rules 2024; Environment (Construction and Demolition) Waste Management Rules 2025. Radioactive waste is separately covered under the Atomic Energy Act 1962 (AERC).
- **3.5** This Section's provisions (clause 4) apply to municipal solid waste only — excludes hazardous chemical waste, bio-medical waste, radioactive waste.

## 4 Solid Waste Management Systems (p. 2692–2695)
- **4.1** Design aim: speedy, efficient conveyance. Stages: sorting/collection/transport → resource recovery (recycling) → resource recovery (energy) → transformation without recovery.
- **4.2 Sorting at Source** (p. 2693): colour-coded bins — **Green** = bio-degradable, **White/Blue** = dry waste, **Red** = bio-medical/rejects. All bins lidded. Manual sorters must have PPE (apron, mask, gloves, boots).
- **4.3 Refuse Chute System** (p. 2693–2695) — for buildings **exceeding 5 storeys**:
  - **4.3.1** Chute internal diameter **≥ 300 mm**. Locate away from living rooms (noise/smell). Inlet hopper near kitchen (individual) or end of common passage (shared); adequate ventilation/lighting; access from well-ventilated common corridor, not opposite flat/lift entries.
  - **4.3.2** Feed openings: hinged, lockable, separate wet/dry receptacles (mixed waste disposal not allowed); wet waste needs leachate collection. Chute features: sprinkler cleaning at every floor intake, **exhaust 25–35 air changes/hour**, fire-resistant discharge door, fire sprinklers inside chute, roof-level vent, direct discharge to trolley. Chute material: masonry, RCC pipe, asbestos cement pipe, or non-corrosive material (stainless steel preferred). Fire rating — see Part F, Fire and Life Safety. Accessibility: chute opening height **≤ 700 mm**, clear space **≥ 1500 mm × 1500 mm** in front, high colour contrast, textured flooring difference for wheelchair users.
  - **4.3.3** Refuse Collection Chamber: ground/basement level, gravity-drained, ramp access, ventilated; floor drain **100 mm dia. trap** with screen to sewer; sized so garbage trolley fits; shutter to keep out scavenging animals.
  - **4.3.4** Refuse Collection Room: ground/basement, ventilated, drained, vehicle/trolley access.
- **4.4 Dumb-Waiter or Service Lift** (p. 2695): for buildings **> 5 storeys**, electrically operated dumb-waiters may carry packaged garbage; garbage chamber at ground/basement per 4.3 provisions.

## 6 Treatment (p. 2696–2698)
- **6.2 Biological Processes**: Aerobic (composting, aerated static pile, in-vessel, vermi-culture → compost/liquid manure) vs Anaerobic (low/high-solids digestion → methane for energy).
- **6.2.1 Composting**: manual (anaerobic pit method, 4–6 months to stabilize), mechanical (enclosed vessel, aerobic+anaerobic), vermi-composting (pits ~**0.1 m² per person**, side walls **0.6–0.75 m** high, shaded, fenced; initiate with 75 mm cow dung layer + 1 kg vermi-compost + 75–100 mm dry leaves, decompose 10–15 days; harvest after 3–6 months).
- **6.3 Thermal Processes**: Combustion (incineration, excess air), Pyrolysis (no oxygen, low temp), Gasification (limited air, high temp).
- **6.4 Land Filling**: engineered "MSW landfills"; considerations — site specs, site selection, site facilities, filling specs. Risks if improperly managed: groundwater/surface water contamination, odour/pests, methane generation, bird strikes near airports, fires, slope instability, greenhouse gas release.
- **6.7–6.9**: Households/communities and commercial clusters (markets, malls) encouraged toward home/community composting, bio-methanation.

## Annex B — Treatment of Food Waste (p. 2702–2703)
Avg. **60 g food waste per meal served**. Storage room: ventilated, **6 air changes/hour**, cooled to **24°C** in hot climates, cleaned daily. Methods: feeding to animals (farms/zoos/piggeries), industrial use (soap/cosmetics/biodiesel), anaerobic digestion (biogas), food waste drying systems, "food cyclers" (sterilize at **82°C**, reduce volume **85–93%**).
