# Part D, Section 4 — Acoustics, Sound Insulation and Noise Control

Covers planning against outdoor and indoor noise, acceptable noise levels, and sound insulation requirements by occupancy type (residential, educational, hospital, office, hotel, industrial, laboratory, and miscellaneous buildings), plus noise barrier design and construction-noise control. PDF pages 2131–2202 (2203–2204 already belong to the next section, Part D Sec 5 — Lifts).

## 1 Scope & 2 Terminology (p. 2135–2140)
Standard acoustics terminology: ambient/background noise, decibel, A-weighted sound pressure level (LpA), reverberation time (T), sound reduction index (R), weighted sound reduction index (Rw), level difference (D, Dw, DnT, DnT,w), impact sound pressure level (Ln, L'nT, L'nT,w), NRC, octave/third-octave bands, etc. (p. 2135–2139).

## 3 Planning and Design Against Outdoor Noise (p. 2140–2147)
- **3.2 Traffic noise levels** — Table 1: aircraft flyover noise (Boeing 737 = 107 EPN dB at 450 m) (p. 2140). Table 2: rail noise at 30 m (steam 85 dBA, diesel 83, electric 77) (p. 2140). Table 3: road traffic noise at 30 m by volume/speed, 65–77 dBA (p. 2140).
- **3.3** Comply with outdoor noise regulations in force (Annex D) (p. 2142).
- **3.4.1 Air traffic** — NEF > 40 unacceptable, NEF < 25 acceptable, 25–40 subjective complaints; **no residential development beyond NEF 35** (p. 2142).
- **3.4.3.3 Road traffic zoning** — external LA10 limited to max 70 dBA (sealed windows) / 60 dBA (open windows) for new urban residential development (p. 2143).
- **3.4.4 Construction noise** — source control + propagation-path barriers; schedule/sequence noisy activities with regard to regulation (p. 2143–2144).
- **3.6 Green belts** — min 50–70 m between buildings and railway tracks for relief; only belts > 30 m thick give real value (p. 2144).
- **3.7–3.8 Highway noise barriers** — a well-designed 3 m barrier protects open space; barriers 4–5 m for residential/educational/hospital buildings on city outskirts; up to 10 dBA reduction at ground level close behind a barrier; barriers ineffective beyond ~250 m from road (p. 2144–2145).
- **3.9** Special problems → expert advice (Annex G).

## 4 Planning and Design Against Indoor Noise (p. 2145–2147)
**Table 4 — Acceptable indoor noise levels (dBA)** (p. 2146, Clause 4.1):
| Location | dBA |
|---|---|
| Auditoria, concert halls | 20–25 |
| Radio/TV studios | 20–25 |
| Cinemas | 25–30 |
| Music rooms | 25–30 |
| Hospitals | 35–40 |
| Apartments, hotels, homes | 35–40 |
| Conference rooms, small offices, libraries | 35–40 |
| Court rooms, classrooms | 40–45 |
| Large public offices, banks, stores | 45–50 |
| Restaurants | 50–55 |

4.3.1 Hearing-impaired provisions: low reverberation times, induction loops in noisy counters/conference halls/auditoria/classrooms/cinemas (p. 2146). 4.4 Constructional sound-insulation measures for non-industrial buildings → Annex H.

## 5 Residential Buildings (p. 2147–2149)
**Table 5 — Sound insulation between rooms (airborne), Rw** (p. 2148, Clause 5.2.3.1): between living rooms of different flats = 50 dB; elsewhere between houses/flats = 45 dB; between rooms in same house/flat = 35 dB.
- **5.2.3.5 Impact noise** — floor above a bedroom/living room: impact sound pressure level (L'nT,w) ≤ 60 dB; a 150 mm concrete floor + 12 mm thick carpet typically satisfies this (p. 2148).
- 5.2.2.1 Setback guidance: bedroom windows facing roads with 80–90 dBA traffic noise → building ~30 m from road (45 m+ preferred); at right angles → 15–25 m (p. 2147–2148).

## 6 Educational Buildings (p. 2149–2151)
**Table 6 — Reverberation times in schools (s, at 500 Hz)** (p. 2151, Clause 6.2.3): Assembly halls 1.0–1.25 (occupied) / 1.5–2.0 (empty max); Music rooms 0.75–1.25 / 1.5; Gymnasia/pools — / 1.5; Dining rooms — / 1.25; Classrooms 0.6 / 1.1; Staff rooms 0.5–1.0 / 1.0. Schools for visually impaired: empty classroom ≤ 1.0 s; for hearing impaired ≤ 0.5 s (p. 2150).
- **6.2.4.1 Room classes A–E** by noise sensitivity (workshops/kitchens/gyms = A; assembly/music/typing = B; general classrooms/labs/offices = C; libraries/studies = D; medical/staff rooms = E) (p. 2151).
- **6.2.4.2** Min sound reduction (Dw) between same-class rooms: Class A = 25 dB; C or D = 35 dB; B or E = 45 dB (p. 2152). Between different classes generally 45 dB, with workshops needing >45 dB from any other class.
- **6.2.4.5 Impact noise** — floor above teaching rooms: L'nT,w ≤ 70 dB (p. 2152).

## 7 Hospital Buildings (p. 2152–2154)
- **7.3.4.1** Walls/partitions between rooms: Rw ≥ 45 dB normally, ≥ 50 dB where a noisy room adjoins one needing quiet (p. 2154).
- **7.3.5** Consulting/waiting room isolation: Dw = 45 dBA; needs two doors separated by a lobby/corridor, not a single communicating door (p. 2154).

## 8 Office Buildings (p. 2154–2159)
- **8.3.2.1** Reverberation time ≤ 1.0 s general offices; ≤ 0.75 s small private offices; canteens ≤ 1.0 s (p. 2155).
- **8.3.2.4.1 Open-plan office data-security acoustics** (new provision this revision): full-height sound-absorptive barriers in high-security zones, uniform speech-masking noise, acoustic zoning for confidential work, treatment of reflective surfaces, controlled/predictable AC noise (p. 2156–2157).
- **8.3.3** Recommended sound isolation (Dw): quiet rooms on quiet site = 45 dB; quiet rooms on noisy site = 40 dB; clerical offices = 30 dB (p. 2158). Min Rw for floors = 45 dB with resilient finish (p. 2159).

## 9 Hotels and Hostels (p. 2159–2161)
- **9.2.2.1** Partitions between guest rooms/corridors/floors: not less than 230 mm plastered brick wall or equivalent (Rw = 50 dB) (p. 2160).

## 10 Industrial Buildings (p. 2160–2168)
**Table 7 — Permissible steady-state noise exposure** (p. 2162–2163, Clause 10.3.1): 85 dBA → 16h00; 90 dBA → 8h00; 95 dBA → 4h00; 100 dBA → 2h00; 105 dBA → 1h00; 110 dBA → 0h30; **115 dBA is the absolute ceiling regardless of duration/dose formula**. Formula for other levels: T = 16 / 2^[0.2(L−85)] (p. 2163).
- 10.5 Noise control in specific occupancies (new subclauses): manufacturing/heavy machinery, chemical/pharma, food processing, power plants — each with source isolation/enclosure/barrier guidance (p. 2166–2168).
- Vibration isolation guidance (springs vs rubber vs felt/cork pads), Fig. 1/Fig. 2 for natural-frequency/transmissibility relationships (p. 2164–2166).

## 11 Laboratories and Test Houses (p. 2168–2169)
Sound isolation Dw ≥ 35 dB between offices attached to a lab and the lab itself (p. 2169). Double-glazed windows for sound insulation need ≥ 100 mm gap between panes (p. 2169).

## 12 Miscellaneous Buildings (p. 2169–2172)
- **12.1.1** Law courts: wall between corridor and courtroom Rw ≥ 50 dB (e.g. 230 mm brick) (p. 2169).
- **12.2.3** Libraries: enclosing walls Rw ≥ 50 dB (p. 2170).
- **12.3.2–12.3.3 Auditoria** — Table 8, max SPL from external/mechanical noise by octave band (concert halls "dBA−25" descending to 13 dB at 8kHz; drama/cinema "dBA−30" to 18 dB) (p. 2170). Envelope sound reduction Rw ≈ 65 dB (concert hall) or 55–60 dB (theatre), roof allowed 5–10 dB less (p. 2171).
- **12.4 Cinemas** — Table 9, wall between two screens: Rw by octave band from 38 dB (63 Hz) up to 61 dB (500 Hz) (p. 2171).

## 13–15 Building Services Noise, Neighbourhood Shielding, Traffic Barriers (p. 2172–2174)
- **14 Neighbourhood acoustic shielding during infrastructure development** (new clause this revision) — pre-development noise assessment, noise mitigation plan, temporary barriers ≥ 2.5 m achieving ≥ 10 dBA reduction, vegetative buffers ≥ 5 m wide, 24 h corrective-action window on exceedance, post-construction assessment (p. 2172–2174).
- **15** Barrier surface density > 20 kg/m² → 5 dB reduction if it breaks line of sight, +1.5 dB per additional metre of height (p. 2174).

## Annexes (p. 2174–2202)
- **Annex A** — Noise calculation formulas (combining/subtracting levels, composite partitions, A-weighting Table 10) (p. 2174–2177).
- **Annex B** — Noise Rating (NR) method, Table 11 (NR0–NR75 by octave band) and Table 12 (a,b constants) (p. 2178–2179). NR ≈ dBA − 6.
- **Annex C** — Sound insulation specification/measurement theory (D, DnT, R, Ln, L'nT etc.), Table 13 summary of indices (p. 2179–2181).
- **Annex D** — Outdoor noise regulations in India (reference to prevailing government notifications, not fixed values in this Section) (p. 2181).
- **Annex E** — **NEW: integration of noise barriers/abatement in zonal & urban planning** — noise-sensitive zones (NSZ), noise-generating zones (NGZ), buffer zones, noise mapping requirements for municipal zonal plans (p. 2182–2183).
- **Annex F** — **NEW: noise barrier design & performance evaluation** — min density 20 kg/m² (F-2.1); insertion loss 5–12 dB for 3–6 m barriers (F-3.3); barriers > 3 m thick = "thick barriers" (F-3.4); Kurze–Anderson insertion-loss model (F-3.8); performance categories Table 14 (DLα,NRD absorption, A0–A5) and Table 15 (DLR insulation, B0–B3, B3 required for most highways) (p. 2183–2187).
- **Annex G** — Special-advice-needed problems: acoustic test rooms, performing spaces, broadcast studios, aircraft noise, ground-borne noise, low-frequency noise, active noise control, noise surveys (p. 2187–2189).
- **Annex H** — **Table 16** (walls/partitions, DnT,w 26–60 dB by construction type — steel/plywood/blockwork/timber-stud/masonry, with mass-per-unit-area values) and **Table 17** (floors, DnT,w/L'nT,w by construction) — **verify against PDF p. 2192–2197 before using specific construction assemblies for design; these are long descriptive tables spanning multiple pages and dense with numeric sub-values (kg/m², mm) that risk being mis-ordered by automated text extraction.**
- **Annex J** — HVAC noise-control design techniques (duct velocity ≤ 7.5 m/s near critical areas, expansion angle ≤ 15°, vibration isolation for ≥ 15 m from isolated equipment), Room Criteria (RC) rating method, Tables 18–19 (sound paths/mitigation) (p. 2198–2201).
- **Annex K** — Suggested equipment noise data sheet format for procurement specs (p. 2201).
- **List of Standards** — IS 11050 (Parts 1 & 2):2023 (airborne/impact sound insulation rating), IS 4954:1968 (noise abatement in town planning), IS 2526:1963 (acoustical design of auditoriums), IS 15575 (Parts 1 & 2) (sound level meters) (p. 2202).
