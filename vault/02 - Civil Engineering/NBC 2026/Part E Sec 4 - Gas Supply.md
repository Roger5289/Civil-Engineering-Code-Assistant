# Part E, Section 4 — Gas Supply

Covers safety requirements for piped gas systems in buildings — LPG cylinder installations, LPG bulk storage, piped natural gas (PNG), and medical gas pipeline systems (MGPS). Scope excludes safety rules for gas-burning appliances themselves (p. 2711).

## 1 Scope
Covers safety of persons/property for all piping and gas types (fuel, lighting, medical) in buildings. Does not cover gas-burning appliance safety rules (Cl 1.2). (p. 2711)

## 2 Terminology
Key defined terms: Appliance Valve, Approved Agency, Authority Having Jurisdiction, Competent Authority, Distribution/Distributing Company, Gas Fitter, Manifold, Pilot, Pressure Regulator, Purge, Qualified Installing Agency, Riser, Service Pipe, Service Shut-Off Valve, Vent Pipe. (p. 2711–2712)

## 3 Pressure Regulations
- Pressure regulator required in service pipe wherever supply pressure exceeds appliance operating pressure. Typical upstream/downstream pressures: 400 kN/m² upstream → 2.1 kN/m² (domestic) or 10/30/200 kN/m² (commercial). (Cl 3.1, p. 2712)
- Indoor regulators: rupture vent pipe must terminate ≥1 m above the topmost storey (Cl 3.2(a)); gas cutoff triggers if outlet pressure falls below 50% or rises above 2× operating pressure (Cl 3.2(b)); backup device cuts in at 7 kN/m² (Cl 3.2(c)). (p. 2712)

## 4 Service Shut-Off Valves
- Required on all new/replacement services, readily accessible (Cl 4.1). Located upstream of meter (no regulator) or upstream of regulator (Cl 4.2). Services >7 kN/m² need an approved shut-off valve outside the building (Cl 4.3). Underground valves in a marked covered curb box/manhole/vault (Cl 4.4). (p. 2712)

## 6 Rules for Turning Gas On
Only authorized gas company personnel may turn on gas at a shut-off/multi-consumer valve (Cl 6.1). Several conditions block turning gas on without specific permission — known leaks, uninspected piping, authority hold, unknown-reason shutoff (Cl 6.2); never during a fire. (p. 2712–2713)

## 7 Rules for Shutting Off Gas
Gas fitter must shut off and keep off for: authority order, leak sufficient to risk fire/explosion/asphyxiation, hazardous appliance installation, any condition threatening supply interruption (Cl 7.1). Consumers must be notified before planned shutoff (Cl 7.2–7.3). (p. 2713)

## 8 Installation of Gas Pipes
- Only qualified installing agency may install/repair/replace (Cl 8.1).
- Piping material: wrought iron, steel, copper, stainless steel, or cast iron below 7 kN/m²; cast iron prohibited above that (Cl 8.2.1). SS316/304/321 and flexible PE-coated pipe permitted in low-pressure systems (Cl 8.2.1.1). (p. 2713–2714)
- **Minimum pipe size: 8 mm** (Cl 8.2.2.2). Bends: radius ≥ 5× pipe diameter (Cl 8.2.3). (p. 2714)
- Colour coding: silver-grey with 150 mm red band generally; canary yellow for natural gas (Cl 8.3). (p. 2714)
- Underground piping: minimum burial depth **1 m**, corrosion protection required (coating + cathodic protection) (Cl 8.4). (p. 2714)
- Distance from electrical wiring: **≥ 60 mm**, gas piping generally routed above electrical wiring (below it if the gas is heavier than air) (Cl 8.12.1). Distance from parallel steam piping: **≥ 150 mm**, gas piping preferably below steam piping (Cl 8.13). (p. 2715–2716)
- Fire-stopping required at wall/floor penetrations — proprietary seals, non-combustible sleeving, or listed fire-stop materials; cross-refs **Part F Fire and Life Safety** (Cl 8.7, p. 2715).
- Piping through solid floors: laid in accessible covered channels (Cl 8.7.1). Single pipe without joint for wall crossings (Cl 8.7.2). Gas pipe shall not be bent — use fittings (Cl 8.8). Concealed piping generally not allowed except per underground rules (Cl 8.9). (p. 2715–2716)
- Drips required wherever moisture/condensate can accumulate (Cl 8.10). Piping must be electrically continuous and earthed, except where cathodic protection is used — never used to earth electrical equipment (Cl 8.12). Piping must be gastight; no smoking while working on gas piping (Cl 8.14–8.15). (p. 2715–2716)
- Sloped piping/drips (Cl 8.17): slope ≥ 1/6.3 mm per 4572 mm; drips at condensate-collection points; sediment traps at appliance inlet (Fig. 1, p. 2716, tee-fitting detail). (p. 2716)
- Shutoff valves (Cl 8.18): not in concealed spaces/furnace plenums; accessible; individual valve per meter, per tenant (multi-tenant buildings), per appliance; identification tags required; lab gas outlets need dedicated, signed, accessible valves. (p. 2716–2717)
- Over-pressure protection devices (Cl 8.19): required where appliances operate ≤3.5 kPa but supply exceeds 13.8 kPa (or per manufacturer spec above that). Types: pressure relief valve, monitoring regulator, series regulator, automatic shutoff device (Cl 8.19(c)). (p. 2717–2718)
- Engine/gas turbine powered equipment: flexible (not rigid) gas connections required to absorb vibration/movement (Cl 8.20). (p. 2718)

## 9 Inspection of Services
New/extended gas piping must be inspected, tested, and certified by the Authority before use (Cl 9.1). Tightness test: hold ≥ **20 kN/m²** for ≥ **10 min** with no drop (Cl 9.1.1); above 7 kN/m² supply, withstand **0.6 MN/m² for 4 h** (Cl 9.1.2). Test medium: city gas, air, or inert gas only — never oxygen or liquid. (p. 2718)

## 10 Leakage Check
No open flame/ignition source for leak checks — use soap-and-water solution or a gas detector (Cl 10.2, 10.8). Electric torches only, don't operate switches while searching (Cl 10.3). Meter-dial watch or manometer/gauge method acceptable (Cl 10.4–10.5: no discernible pressure drop over 3 min). Full purge required after checks, never into an appliance's combustion chamber (Cl 10.6–10.7). (p. 2719)

## 11 Use of Liquefied Petroleum Gas (LPG)
- Cylinders per accepted standard [E-4(3)] (Cl 11.1). LPG never transferred between containers (Cl 11.3.1.4). (p. 2719)
- **Fire extinguishers for LPG cylinder installations** (Cl 11.3.1.3, p. 2719–2720):

| LPG Quantity | Number | Type | Capacity |
|---|---|---|---|
| 40–200 kg | 2 | Dry powder | 9 kg |
| >200–320 kg | 3 | Dry powder | 9 kg |
| >320 kg–1000 kg | 4 | Dry powder | 9 kg |

Plus two sand buckets + two water buckets nearby; electrical installations also need one 4.5 kg CO₂ extinguisher.

- **Cylinder location limits** (Cl 11.3.2.1, p. 2720–2721): ≤50 kg indoor on any floor (min. 5 m² floor area); multiple 50 kg units need 3 m spacing, 1 installation/5 m², aggregate ≤200 kg; ≤100 kg needs ≥12 m² floor area; ≤400 kg needs a dedicated ventilated room; 400 kg (or 200 kg without a dedicated room) up to 1000 kg must be outdoors, ground floor only, 3 m clearance from buildings/roads; installations >1000 kg go in 1000 kg multiples with manifolds, max 4 installations (incl. standby), 3 m inter-spacing, ≤30 cylinders/installation.
- Cylinders >20 kg not permitted above ground floor (Cl 11.3.2.1(k)). Not under stairways, in basements/cellars, or near steam pipes/boilers/transformers/DG sets (Cl 11.3.2.1(q)(5)). ≥1 m from culverts/depressions/drains (Cl 11.3.2.1(n)). Public-place/commercial/industrial installs: ≤400 kg indoor aggregate; 400 kg–1000 kg needs outdoor covered shed, two installations ≥3 m apart; >1000 kg per the general multiple-of-1000kg rule. (p. 2721)
- Cylinder storage room construction: non-flammable (100 mm concrete / 230 mm brick / 5 mm MS angle steel frame + 1.63 mm wire mesh); floor-level ventilation (LPG is heavier than air) with min. 500×300 mm double-mesh vents; ≥25% of wall area as openings; outward-opening louvered doors; no flammable storage. (Cl 11.3.2.1(r), p. 2721–2722)
- Portable installations: ≤100 kg/manifold, ≤200 kg total per workspace; trolley-mounted must be stable/secured (Cl 11.3.2.2). (p. 2722)
- Manifold requirements: components withstand test pressure ≥2.5 N/mm² (or 1.5× developed pressure at 65°C) (Cl 11.3.3.2); ≥300 mm gap between manifold and cylinder valve protection ring; ≥400 mm between connection nipples (or cylinder diameter + 100 mm if larger); 100 mm pressure gauge, range 0–1.0 N/mm² (Cl 11.3.3.1–11.3.3.10). (p. 2722–2723)

- Protection: ≥2 m industrial fence around tanks/vaporizers/pumping/loading area, ≥2 exits, outward-opening non-self-locking gates (Cl 11.4.3.1). Vegetation/combustibles cleared 3 m (tanks ≤2000 ℓ) or 6 m (larger) from tank shell (Cl 11.4.4). No smoking/naked flames in safety zone (Cl 11.4.5). ## 12 Use of Piped Natural Gas (PNG)
- Must comply with PNGRB (Technical Standards and Specifications incl. Safety Standards for City/Local Natural Gas Distribution Network) Regulations, 2008 — and that regulation **prevails over this NBCS in case of conflict** (Cl 12.1, 12.3.6). (p. 2725)
- Society/builder must engage the City Gas Distribution (CGD) entity at design stage; infrastructure must stay accessible to CGD; no structures over underground gas pipeline routes (Cl 12.1.1–12.1.3). (p. 2725–2726)
- Pipeline networks: min. **1 m top cover**, no PE pipe through basements (Cl 12.3.1); **≥300 mm clearance** from other services (Cl 12.3.2); minimum proximity to properties: **0.25 m** at ≤0.01 N/mm², **3.00 m** at ≤0.4 N/mm² (Cl 12.3.3). (p. 2726)
- Riser/lateral systems: buildings with **≥8 floors** should have dedicated utility shafts/ducts for the riser (Cl 12.5.2); CGD needs safe access, concrete slab at each floor in continuous shafts (Cl 12.5.3). (p. 2726–2727)

- Natural ventilation only (mechanical ventilation not accepted) for gas voids (Cl 12.5.4.1). Single-ventilator ducts: free area ≥0.04 m² or 4% of CSA, whichever greater (Cl 12.5.4.2). All flammable release sources below the lowest high-level vent opening (Cl 12.5.4.7). Gas pipes may share a duct with most other services (water, electrical, other fuel pipes) EXCEPT sub-atmospheric vacuum/vent ducts not fully welded/brazed, and oxidizing/corrosive-fluid services (Cl 12.5.4.8). (p. 2727)
- Clearances inside ducts: **≥300 mm** from electrical equipment/fuse boxes, **≥25 mm** from electrical cables/other metal services (Cl 12.5.5). (p. 2727–2728)
- Kitchen/in-premise piping: meter on an outside wall, kitchen preferred location (Cl 12.6.1); **min. 2% of room floor area** as ventilation where the meter sits (Cl 12.6.2); floor-laid pipe needs ≥75 mm screed for ≥50 mm cover (Cl 12.6.3); not routed through cavity walls — sleeved where unavoidable (Cl 12.6.4). (p. 2728)

## 13 Use of Medical Gas Pipeline System (MGPS)
- Distributes oxygen, nitrous oxide, medical/surgical air, medical vacuum; may include anaesthetic gas scavenging. No cross-connection permitted between gas-specific systems (Cl 13.1). Governed by the Indian Drugs and Cosmetics Act, 1940. (p. 2728)
- Separate Laboratory Gases (Pipeline) Supply System (LGSS) for pathology/labs — valve-controlled outlets only, never medical gas terminal units (Cl 13.1). (p. 2728)
- Design/install/test per good practice [E-4(11)] for compressed gas/vacuum pipelines, [E-4(12)] for anaesthetic gas scavenging disposal systems (Cl 13.2). (p. 2728–2729)
