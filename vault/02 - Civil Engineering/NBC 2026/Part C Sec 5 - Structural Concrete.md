# Part C, Section 5 — Structural Concrete

Covers structural design of plain, reinforced and prestressed concrete (PDF pp. 797–852). Based on IS 456:2000 and IS 1343:2012 (being merged into a future single "Structural Concrete" standard) plus IS 16700:2023 for tall buildings. This edition merges the old 5A (plain/RC) and 5B (prestressed) subsections into one Section 5, and folds special concretes (self-compacting, high-performance, steel-fibre) into annexes, plus a new tall-building safety annex.

## Main body (Clauses 1–6, p. 801)

- **1 Scope** (p. 801) — plain, reinforced and prestressed concrete structural use.
- **2 Terminology** (p. 801) — per accepted standards [C-5(1)].
- **3 Materials** (p. 801) — must conform to Part B "Building Materials".
- **4 Structural design using plain, reinforced and prestressed concrete** (p. 801) — materials/workmanship/testing, general design, special member/system requirements, and limit-state design all deferred to IS 456 (good practice C-5(2)) and IS 1343 (C-5(3)). Note: once the merged revised IS 456 publishes, it supersedes this clause.
- **5 Structural safety of tall RC buildings** (p. 801) — for RC buildings >50 m and ≤250 m (residential/office/commercial), see **Annex E** and IS 16700:2023.
- **6 Earthquake-resistant design and detailing** (p. 801) — per IS 13920 [C-5(5)].

## Annex A — Materials for Making Concrete (informative, p. 802)

- **Aggregates**: normal weight 22.40–27.00 kN/m³; heavyweight >27.00 up to 45.00 kN/m³ (radiation shielding, counterweights); lightweight 14.40–18.40 kN/m³ (sintered fly ash for light-weight structural concrete). (p. 802)
- **Cement types permitted**: OPC, Portland slag, Portland pozzolana (fly ash/calcined clay based), composite cement (min. 45% clinker, ≤25% fly ash, min 43 MPa 28-day strength), Portland calcined-clay-limestone (barred in underground/groundwater-contact structures where temps stay <15°C for 6 months). Special-use cements: sulphate-resisting, microfine OPC, hydrophobic, low-heat, rapid-hardening, high-alumina, supersulphated, white. (p. 802–803)
- **Water** (p. 803–804): potable water needs no testing if chloride ≤500 ppm, alkalinity ≤25 ml. Other sources (surface, treated, recovered process water, recycled, desalinated) must pass **Table 1** and **Table 2** limits. Sea/brackish water is barred.
  - **Table 1 — Preliminary Inspection Requirements** (p. 804): oils/fats "not more than visible traces"; pH 6.0–8.5; foam must disappear ≤2 min; colour/odour/humic-matter qualitative checks.
  - **Table 2 — Max Permissible Limits for Solids** (p. 805): organic 200 mg/l (both curing & mixing); inorganic 3,000 mg/l; sulphate 400 mg/l (curing) / 800 mg/l (mixing); chloride 2,000 mg/l plain / 500 mg/l RC (curing) and 500 mg/l RC-PSC / 2,000 mg/l plain (mixing), with a 1,000 mg/l RC relaxation allowed if hardened-concrete chloride content is separately verified; suspended matter 2,000 mg/l.
- **Admixtures** (p. 805–807): mineral/SCMs — fly ash ≤35% of cementitious material (min fineness 280 m²/kg, lime reactivity ≥4.5 N/mm²); silica fume 3–10%; combined fly ash+silica fume ≤35% for RCC; GGBS+silica fume/UFGBS ≤60% for RCC; metakaolin ≤15%; GGBS alone ≤50% (≤60% for sulphate/AAR resistance); UFGBS ≤20%; ultrafine fly ash ≤20%. Combined fly ash+GGBS blend cap: total ≤45%, fly ash ≤20%, GGBS ≤25%.
  - **Table 3 — Chemical Admixture Types & Constituents** (p. 807–808): air-entraining, retarding, accelerating, water-reducing, high-range water-reducing, shrinkage-reducing, corrosion-inhibiting, permeability-reducing (non-hydrostatic PRAN / hydrostatic PRAH) — each with typical active constituents listed.
  - Shrinkage-compensating admixtures are **barred** in structural concrete (A-4.3.4).
- **Fibres** (p. 808) — need competent-authority approval via peer review; strength contribution can't be counted unless per Annex D.
- **Reinforcement** (p. 809) — bars/strands per IS 456/1343; couplers per [C-5(33)]; GFRP bars per [C-5(34)]; welded wire fabric per [C-5(35)].

## Annex B — Self-Compacting Concrete (SCC) (p. 809–813)

- Used for congested reinforcement, restricted-access placement, tunnel linings, CFST columns.
- **Slump-flow classes**: SF1 550–650mm (slabs, pump-injection, piles); SF2 650–750mm (walls, columns — general use); SF3 750–850mm (congested reinforcement, complex shapes, aggregate <20mm).
- **Passing ability** (L-box, blocking ratio h2/h1): 0.8–1.0.
- **Segregation resistance** (sieve test): SR1 15–<20%; SR2 <15%.
- **Viscosity** (V-funnel): V1 <8s; V2 8–<25s.
- Engineering properties: similar compressive/tensile strength to conventional concrete; Ec may be slightly lower (matters for prestressing camber control); creep coefficient may be slightly higher; low permeability improves durability.

## Annex C — High Performance Concrete (p. 813–815)

Table (p. 813) of performance requirements and applications: carbonation resistance (semi-arid service life), chloride resistance (coastal/marine), abrasion resistance (spillways, pavements), impact resistance (blast structures), flexure/shear improvement (bridge girders, shear walls), shrinkage-cracking resistance (repair mortars). Test methods: accelerated carbonation (3–4% CO₂, 70 days), RCPT/air permeability/electrical resistivity for QA, chloride diffusion (3% NaCl, 90 days ponding/immersion), revolving-disc/underwater-abrasion tests, falling-weight impact test.

## Annex D — Steel Fibre Reinforced Concrete (SFRC) Design (p. 815–829)

- Classified by compressive strength (f'c) and characteristic residual tensile strength (f'1.5): standard grades 0.4/0.6/0.8/1.2/1.6/2.0 MPa (p. 816).
- Fire: residual tensile stress reduces with temperature — **Table 4** (p. 826): factor kθ1 = 1.0 at 0–100°C, 0.6 at 500°C, 0.1 at 700°C, 0.0 at 1200°C (linear interpolation permitted).
- Design equations given for shear contribution (Vuf = 0.7·kθ·bv·d0·f'1.5, p. 823), minimum shear reinforcement, minimum crack-control reinforcement, short/long-term deflection multiplier kcs.
- Durability: SFRC **not permitted** in exposure classes C1/C2 (p. 828); fibres near the surface may stain but don't affect durability.
- Production/QA: **Table 5** pre-construction tests (p. 827), **Table 6** routine production control (p. 828) — dosing equipment checked once/production day, fibre delivery checked every delivery, fibre content every batch, finished-product flexural test 2 beams every other production day. **Table 7** acceptance criteria (p. 829): single sample ≥0.80 of target dosage; 3-sample average ≥0.85; continuous control average (>3 tests) ≥0.90.

## Annex E — Structural Safety of Tall Concrete Buildings (p. 829–848)

Applies to RC buildings **>50 m and ≤250 m** (>250 m = "Super Tall", out of scope), occupancy ≤20,000 persons, not within 10 km of a seismogenic fault (near-field exclusion).

- **Structural systems covered**: structural wall, moment frame, moment frame+wall, wall+flat-slab+perimeter frame, wall+framed-tube, framed-tube, tube-in-tube, multiple-tube, hybrid, plus outrigger/belt-truss variants.
- **Table 8 — Max height H (m) above base level, by structural system & seismic zone** (p. 833). Five systems, columns in order: Moment Frame · Structural Wall (Well Distributed) · Wall + Moment Frame · Wall + Perimeter Frame · Wall + Framed Tube.
  - **Zone V:** NA · 120 · 150 · 150 · 180
  - **Zone IV:** NA · 150 · 200 · 200 · 225
  - **Zone III:** 60 · 200 · 225 · 225 · 250
  - **Zone II:** 80 · 250 · 250 · 250 · 250
  - Moment-frame-only is not admissible (NA) in Zones IV and V. "Well distributed" = walls outside the core carry ≥ 25 % of lateral load. *(Corrected 2026-09-05 — the earlier draft mis-labelled the Zone V 180 m as "wall+perimeter-frame"; it is wall+framed-tube. Wall+perimeter-frame in Zone V is 150 m.)*
- **Table 9 — Max slenderness ratio (Ht/Bt)** (p. 833–834): Zone V/IV moment-frame not admissible; wall systems 8–9; Zone III moment frame 4, walls 8–10; Zone II moment frame 5, walls 9–10.
- Plan aspect ratio (Lt/Bt) ≤5.0 (p. 834); plan preferably rectangular/elliptical.
- Storey stiffness ≥70% and strength ≥90% of storey above (p. 834).
- **Lateral drift**: inter-storey drift ratio ≤1/500 under serviceability wind (20-yr return); total drift at top ≤H/500; single storey relaxation to hi/400 (p. 834).
- Torsional natural period ≤0.9× smaller translational period; translational period ≤8s (p. 834–835).
- Floor systems: cast-in-situ slabs; precast needs ≥75mm topping mesh (Zone III+) or ≥50mm (Zone II) (p. 835). Diaphragm openings: none at edges unless perimeter members verified; max 30% of plan area; min residual slab width 5m without perimeter beam. Floor natural frequency ≥3 Hz.
- **Materials**: min grade M30, max grade M70 (higher needs experimental verification of ≥0.002 min crushing strain) (p. 836). Bar laps avoided in seismic columns/walls for ≥20mm dia — use mechanical couplers instead.
- **Wind**: site-specific tunnel study required if height >150m, irregular plan/elevation, complex topography, or natural period >5s (p. 836). **Table 10 — Max peak combined acceleration** (10-yr return, p. 836): residential 0.18 m/s², mercantile 0.25 m/s². Damping ≤2% critical.
- **Seismic**: vertical shaking considered with horizontal in Zone V. **Table 11 — Min design base shear coefficient (% seismic weight)** (p. 837): H≤120m — Zone II 0.7%, III 1.1%, IV 1.6%, V 2.4%; H≥200m — 0.5/0.75/1.25/1.75% respectively (interpolate 120–200m).
- Approximate fundamental period: Ta = 0.0644·H^0.9 (moment frame) or 0.0672·H^0.75 (other systems) (p. 837).
- **Structural analysis**: 3D computer model required; **Table 12 — Cracked section properties** (p. 838): slabs 0.35Ig (serviceability)/0.25Ig (strength); beams 0.7Ig/0.35Ig; columns & walls 0.9Ig/0.70Ig. P-Δ load combo: 1.2DL+0.5IL±1.5EL/WL. Inter-storey stability coefficient θ <0.2.
- **Staircase (clause E-8.1.2, p. 839)**: staircases built integral with the structure and not confined by structural walls must be included in the 3D structural model and designed for the forces induced under all load combinations — **or** a sliding joint provided instead.
- **Tall buildings on common podium**: backstay-effect sensitivity analysis required (**Table 13**, p. 840: stiffness upper/lower bound 0.5/0.15 for both moment of inertia and area); backstay diaphragm min 150mm thick, double-mesh ≥0.25% each direction (p. 841); seismic force amplification ×1.5 for irregular diaphragms, ×2.5 for Type III diaphragm discontinuity (p. 841).
- **Structural wall requirements**: min thickness 160mm or hi/20 (p. 844); Zone IV+ walls continuous to base, min longitudinal reinf. 0.4% gross area, min transverse 0.25%, two curtains each direction, no random unaligned openings (p. 845). **Table 14 — Max coupling-beam longitudinal reinforcement** (p. 845): span/depth ≤1.0 → 0.6%; 1.0–<2.0 → 1.2%; ≥2.0 → 1.5%.
- **Framed-tube systems**: column spacing preferably ≤5m; corner column area 1–2× internal column (p. 845–846). **Table 15 — Beam reinforcement in framed-tube moment frames** (p. 846): stirrup dia >8mm (Zone II) / >10mm (Zone III+); stirrup spacing <150mm/<100mm; main bars >16mm both zones.
- **Foundations** (p. 846–847): factor of safety 1.5 (wind) / 2.5× (seismic) against overturning/sliding. Min embedment 1/15 of height (raft) or 1/20 (pile/piled raft, excluding pile length) — relaxable on hard rock or with no uplift anywhere. Soil-structure interaction study mandatory for rafts of buildings >150m. **Max settlement 125mm (raft/piled-raft, angular distortion ≤1/500) / 50mm (rock)** (p. 847).
- **Monitoring**: tri-axial accelerometers mandatory in Zone V and Zone III/IV buildings >150m (p. 847); anemometers/accelerometers optional >150m for wind; permanent settlement markers at raft top, ≥5 pressure pads or pile strain gauges (p. 848).
