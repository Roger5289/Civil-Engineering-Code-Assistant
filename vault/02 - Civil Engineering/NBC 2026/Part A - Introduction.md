# Part A — Introduction

Scope: how to apply the NBCS as a whole, occupancy/construction-type classification, and — the operative content — who is legally competent to plan, design, supervise, or peer-review what, by building size/height. PDF pages 4–54, but pages 4–45 are shared front matter (Contents, Foreword, and Sectional Committee/Panel rosters common to the whole document — names/organizations only, not reproduced here, not technically substantive). The actual Part A text is pages 46–54 only. No clause numbering scheme is used in the main narrative (unusual for this document — every other Part uses numbered clauses); only Annex A has real clause numbers.

## Scope and approach (p. 48–50)

This Part is guidance on applying the rest of the NBCS with a coordinated, multi-disciplinary team approach across a building's life cycle (planning → design → construction → operation/maintenance → eventual decommissioning/deconstruction). Key points:

- **Occupancy classification** (p. 48): Residential, Educational, Institutional, Assembly, Business, Mercantile, Industrial, Storage, Hazardous.
- **Types of construction** (p. 48): Type 1–4, based on fire resistance rating of components — actual ratings are in **Part F — Fire and Life Safety**, not here.
- **Building parts referenced generically** (p. 49): plinth, habitable rooms, kitchen, bathroom/WC, ledges, mezzanine, store room, garage, basement, parapet, cabin, boundary wall, wells, septic tanks, meter rooms, **staircases**, roofs — this Part only lists these as existing; it sets no dimensional/design requirements for any of them (those live in the relevant technical Part/Section — e.g. staircase requirements are in Part F for egress and would follow structural/material requirements from Part C).
- Safe clearance between buildings and electric lines is flagged as essential (no value given here).
- Accessibility for persons with disabilities and the elderly is flagged as essential in specified public buildings/low-income housing (no specific standard cited here).
- Open spaces within a plot matter for natural light/ventilation and for fire-tender/emergency-vehicle movement; parking should be provided so public roads/right-of-way aren't burdened. State/local authorities set the specific numeric criteria — not this NBCS (explicitly out of scope, see below).
- High-rise/special buildings need periodic **audits** — structural, electrical, fire, accessibility — recommended every 3–5 years (p. 49). No further detail on audit scope/method here.
- Multi-disciplinary project teams: lists 23 possible specialist roles (architect, civil/structural/geotechnical/electrical/plumbing/fire-protection/HVAC engineer, lift specialist, acoustics specialist, ICT engineer, HSE specialist, sustainability specialist, town planner, urban designer, landscape architect, security specialist, interior designer, quantity surveyor, project/construction manager, digitization specialist, accessibility/universal-design specialist, others) (p. 49–50).
- **BIM** (Building Information Modelling) recommended for all major/complex projects (p. 50) — no mandate, no minimum project size given.
- Deconstruction/demolition at end-of-life should be preceded by a planned decommissioning process, ideally documented from the project's early stages (p. 50).

**Explicitly out of scope of this NBCS edition** (p. not clause-numbered, stated in the frontmatter foreword, p.6-7): FAR/FSI, development control rules (DCRs), parking norms, open space/ground coverage/land-use rules — these are state/local subjects. Also removed to separate BIS handbooks: old NBC 2016 Part 7 (Construction Management/Practices/Safety), Part 10 (Landscape/Signs/Outdoor Display), Part 11 (Sustainability), Part 12 (Asset & Facility Management). This edition (2026, called "National Building Construction Standards") keeps only: Part A (this), B Materials, C Structural Design, D Building Services, E Plumbing, F Fire and Life Safety.

## Annex A — Qualification and competence of registered building professionals (p. 51–54)

This is the operative regulatory content of Part A: who is legally allowed to sign off on what, by project size/height.

**A-1 Essential requirement:** every building/development work must be planned, designed and supervised by *registered* professionals, one or more of: architect, engineer, structural engineer, geotechnical engineer, supervisor, town planner, landscape architect, urban designer, utility services engineer.

| Professional | Min. qualification | Competence limit |
|---|---|---|
| **Architect** (A-2.1) | Per Architects Act 1972, registered with Council of Architecture | All plans/info for building permit except engineering services of high-rise/special buildings; supervision/completion certification (architectural aspects); subdivision/layout plans up to 1 ha (metro) / 2 ha (other) |
| **Engineer** (A-2.2) | B.E/B.Tech Civil or Architectural Engg, or Corporate Member IEI (Civil/Arch Div) | Full building-permit plans; structural details/calcs incl. sub-surface investigation **up to 500 m² plot and up to 5 storeys or 16 m height**; supervision/completion cert for all buildings; layout plans up to 1 ha (metro)/2 ha (other); all service plans; supervision cert for land development of any area |
| **Structural Engineer** (A-2.3) | B.E Civil + min. 3 yrs structural design/field experience (2 yrs with PG, 1 yr with PhD in structural engg) | Structural design/calcs/details + supervision for **all** buildings; buildings with "special structural features" beyond A-2.2.1(b)/A-2.5.1(a) limits **must** use a structural engineer |
| **Geotechnical Engineer** (A-2.4) | B.E Civil + min. 3 yrs geotech experience (2 yrs PG, 1 yr PhD) | Sub-surface investigation, foundation type/bearing capacity/depth recommendations; buildings with special geotechnical conditions beyond A-2.2.1(b)/A-2.5.1(a) limits **must** use one; see also IS 19235:2025 for geotech service scope |
| **Supervisor** (A-2.5) | Diploma Civil/Architectural Asst, or equivalent + 5 yrs experience | Residential buildings only, **up to 200 m² plot and 2 storeys or 7.5 m height** |
| **Town Planner** (A-2.6) | AITP or graduate/PG in town & country planning | Layout/subdivision plans, all areas; land-development supervision cert, all areas. *Note:* layouts >5 ha should also involve a landscape architect; utility engineers for roads/water/sewerage/electrification |
| **Landscape Architect** (A-2.7) | Bachelor's/Master's in landscape architecture | Landscape design for **≥5 ha** (≥2 ha in metro cities) |
| **Urban Designer** (A-2.8) | Master's in urban design | Building permit urban design for **>5 ha** land / **>2 ha** campus; urban renewal, all areas |
| **Utility services engineers** (A-2.9) — mechanical/HVAC, electrical, plumbing | Per Part D (Building Services) / Part E (Plumbing Services) requirements | AC/heating/mech-vent, electrical, lifts/escalators, water supply/drainage/sanitation/gas — respectively |
| **Fire protection engineer** (A-2.9, p.51–52) | (a) fire protection/fire engg graduate, OR (b) mech/civil/elec/electronics graduate + PG in fire protection engg, OR (c) same + 10 yrs fire protection experience, OR (d) advance diploma in fire engg (Divisional Officers Course, National Fire Service College Nagpur) + 5 yrs experience, OR (e) diploma from NFSC Nagpur + 10 yrs experience. *Note:* existing professionals with science graduation or engg diploma + 10 yrs experience may also be allowed. | Relevant to Part F — Fire and Life Safety |
| **Builder/Constructor** (A-2.10) | Same qualification tiers as A-2.1–A-2.9 for the relevant trade | Execution work matching the same scope/limits as the corresponding professional's supervision authority |
| **Peer Reviewer / Proof Checker — structural design** (A-2.11) | (a) buildings ≤15 m: B.E/B.Tech Civil + 7 yrs structural practice (≥5 yrs exclusively structural design); (b) buildings >15 m and ≤50 m: same + 10 yrs (≥7 yrs exclusively structural); (c) buildings >50 m or specialized structures: Master's in structural engg + 10 yrs (≥7 yrs exclusively structural), OR B.E/B.Tech Civil + 15 yrs (≥10 yrs exclusively structural); (d) reputed academic/research institutions (IITs, NITs, IISc, CSIR-SERC, CBRI, CRRI etc.) may also carry out peer review | Structural design peer review/proof checking, tiered by building height |
